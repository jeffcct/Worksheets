\begin{center}
  {\Large\bfseries Finding the Equation of a Graph}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.2em}
\noindent $y = mx+c$ (linear, from the gradient and $y$-intercept) \quad\(\cdot\)\quad
$y=a(x-h)^2+k$ (quadratic, from the turning point $(h,k)$) \quad\(\cdot\)\quad
$y=a(x-p)(x-q)$ (quadratic, from the $x$-intercepts $p,q$).
A $y$-intercept, $x$-intercept, or turning point is \textbf{not} marked with a dot --- read it directly from where the graph crosses or turns on the grid. Only the \emph{extra} point needed to find $a$ (or the gradient) is marked with a dot. Find any unknown constant by substituting that point, then write the full equation.
Coordinates are written out for Questions 1--4; for Questions 5--10, the marked point sits exactly on a grid intersection, so read its coordinates from the grid.

\vspace{0.3em}

\item Find the equation of the curve shown.
\begin{center}

\end{axis}
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
\qspace

\item Find the equation of the curve shown.

\begin{tikzpicture}

\end{axis}
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
\qspace

\item Find the equation of the line shown. The triangle marks the gradient (rise over run).
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}
\graphaxis{-1}{3}{-1}{10}{1}

\end{axis}
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
\qspace
